# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Muslims aged roughly 13 to 60 who are comfortable with a smartphone: people new to practice, people returning to it, and people who are already consistent. The interface assumes tech literacy and does not simplify itself for users who struggle with apps. English is the only interface language at launch; Quran text is Arabic.

They open Ayyur several times a day in short sessions: before or after a prayer, at suhoor or iftar, on a commute, and late at night for Quran. The job is to see what is due, record what they did honestly, and keep going tomorrow.

## Product Purpose

Ayyur (ⴰⵢⵢⵓⵔ, "moon" in Tamazight) is an all-in-one Islamic companion for prayer, fasting, Quran, habits and tasks, with an AI companion that works across all of them. It is local-first, free for every core feature, ad-free, and private by design. Success is a user who grows in their practice with dignity and agency, including one who never signs in or pays.

The home screen leads with the next prayer. The companion has its own tab.

## Brand Personality

**Warm, playful, sincere.** Ayyur is a warm encourager: visibly glad when the user shows up, never disappointed when they do not.

- **Mascot.** A moon character named Ayyur, with a simple face (two eyes, a mouth). It celebrates streaks, and it is also the AI companion the user talks to. One character, one name.
- **Playfulness.** Openly playful and mascot-led for streaks, milestones and badges: lively motion, sound and haptics, all individually switchable.
- **Voice.** Natural Muslim English. Salah, wudu, insha'Allah and masha'Allah are used freely and unexplained, the way Muslims speak. Ayyur speaks in the first person, briefly, and never lectures.
- **Heritage.** Amazigh roots appear in the name, the Tifinagh wordmark and subtle woven geometry. Islamic geometric pattern, Arabic calligraphy and architectural forms appear as accents, not wallpaper.
- **References.** Duolingo for streak clarity, chunky controls and character-led celebration. Pillars and Quran.com for clean, ad-free, respectful worship screens.

## Anti-references

- **Cluttered legacy apps.** Muslim Pro-style ads, dense menus, stock mosque photos and green-and-gold gradients.
- **A kids' app.** Anything so cartoonish that an adult would feel embarrassed opening it in public.
- **A generic AI chatbot.** Purple gradients, sparkle icons, a chat box as the whole product.
- **Solemn and heavy.** Dark ornate luxury, gold filigree, a tone that feels like being lectured.
- **Guilt mechanics.** Flames, loss warnings, sad or crying mascots, public rankings, fictitious reward counters, paid streak protection.

## Design Principles

1. **Glad to see you, every time.** Feedback rewards showing up. A missed or unrecorded day is shown neutrally; the mascot never expresses disappointment and nothing is styled as failure.
2. **Worship is a calm zone.** The Quran reader, adhkar and a prayer in progress drop the mascot, counters and celebration. Playfulness returns after the act, never during it.
3. **Honest marks.** Completed, frozen, pending and unrecorded each have their own shape and label. A freeze never looks like a completion, and the app never implies worship happened.
4. **Play that an adult can open in public.** Bold and joyful, with flat shapes, restrained faces and real typography. Delight lives in moments, not on every pixel.
5. **Private in plain words.** Anything that leaves the device, especially AI context, is disclosed where it happens in language that does not overclaim.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG 2.2 AA contrast in both themes: 4.5:1 for body text and 3:1 for large text and essential graphics, including text on module colours.
- Dynamic type: layouts hold at the largest system font sizes, Quran text included.
- System reduce-motion is respected; sound and haptics each have their own switch.
- VoiceOver and TalkBack labels on every control. Status is never carried by colour alone: every state pairs colour with an icon or a word.
- The mascot's face is deliberately simple. It is absent from worship screens for every user.
- Out of scope at launch: right-to-left interface, French, Arabic and Tamazight localisation. Rubik was chosen so Arabic UI can be added later without changing typeface.
